# Learn from investigations and partnerships

Read the <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/one-health/php/stories/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CDC story collection</a> and [EPA collection](https://www.epa.gov/one-health/one-health-case-studies-and-examples), then use the original activities below to examine evidence, geography and collaboration. Four CDC readings and two EPA selections connect to existing notebooks. These are discussion and evidence-planning activities, not additional disease datasets or reconstructions of the investigations.

The [Fresno profile](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-OneHealth/explorer/profile/) contains measured Landsat samples from 2024 and Census population from 2020. Its dates, geography and variables differ from those in these stories. Environmental observations cannot establish an outbreak, individual exposure or intervention effect. The profiler continues to support public-domain U.S. inputs only. The Nigeria reading provides a Global Health comparison rather than a new supported acquisition area.

## Wildlife contact and human illness

**Read:** CDC, NCEZID, <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/one-health/php/stories/investigating-salmonella-and-wild-songbirds-a-one-health-approach.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Investigating Salmonella and Wild Songbirds</a> (page dated 19 April 2024).

The 2020–2021 investigation combined human interviews, laboratory results and wildlife evidence. Partners included health departments, wildlife agencies and laboratory networks.

**Activity:** Open [agency questions](../content/01_agency_questions.ipynb) and [spatial joins](../content/07_eo_population_joins.ipynb). Separate where vegetation was observed, where contact occurred, and where a pathogen was identified. Make an evidence table with a source, date, spatial support and responsible partner for each question.

**Deliverable:** A sampling and interview plan. Explain why a green pixel or tract overlap cannot identify an infected bird, exposed resident or transmission link. Keep sensitive contact and wildlife records outside public teaching files.

## A freshwater-to-coast pathway

**Read:** CDC, NCEZID, <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/one-health/php/stories/poisoned-sea-otters.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Poisoned Sea Otters in California</a> (page dated 16 April 2024).

The investigation began around Monterey Bay in 2007. Investigators identified microcystin and examined the connection between Pinto Lake, downstream waters and sea otters, bringing environmental sampling and veterinary investigation together.

**Activity:** Use [vegetation and water](../content/04_vegetation_water.ipynb) to explain the declared spectral ratios. Design a new U.S. watershed study, keeping the lake, receiving waters and coast distinct. A study box is a selection boundary, not a hydrologic catchment. Identify the independently sourced watershed and flow evidence a later analysis would need.

**Deliverable:** A learner-drawn source-to-receptor diagram and a list of required water, toxin, shellfish and animal-health measurements. A Landsat water proxy cannot determine microcystin concentration or water safety. The Fresno scene does not reproduce the 2007 investigation.

## Animal warnings, livelihoods and equity

**Read:** CDC, NCEZID, <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/one-health/php/stories/lead-poisoning.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Lead Poisoning Investigation in Northern Nigeria</a> (page dated 16 April 2024).

CDC describes the 2010 Zamfara investigation of lead exposure associated with ore processing. Reports about animal deaths became relevant to understanding the shared environmental hazard.

**Activity:** In [equity and evidence gaps](../content/08_equity_evidence_gaps.ipynb), compare the roles of community observations, environmental sampling, clinical findings and livelihood constraints. Ask how residents would help define a useful response and communicate uncertainty.

**Deliverable:** A partner-and-evidence matrix and a Global Health reflection. Do not substitute U.S. Census counts for Nigerian population or infer lead concentration from vegetation, temperature or settlement appearance. This activity imports no Nigerian dataset and produces no exposure estimate.

## Resistance across water systems

**Read:** CDC, NCEZID, <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/one-health/php/stories/understanding-antibiotic-resistance-in-water.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Understanding Antibiotic Resistance in Water</a> (page dated 19 April 2024).

The reading considers resistance through connected human, animal and water environments, with contributions from international experts.

**Activity:** Extend [agency questions](../content/01_agency_questions.ipynb) with an upstream, discharge and downstream sampling design for a U.S. water body. Define the organisms or resistance markers, sampling dates, laboratory methods and flow information needed for comparison.

**Deliverable:** A measurement specification. Explain why water appearance, spectral indices and a residential tract count cannot quantify resistant organisms or the population served by a wastewater system. No resistance observations or sewershed boundaries are bundled.

## Missouri: organizing a shared response

**Read:** EPA, [Showing the Way for One Health Collaboration](https://www.epa.gov/research-states/showing-way-one-health-collaboration) (last updated 29 December 2025).

EPA's Missouri account describes coordination among agriculture, natural-resource, health and conservation agencies, alongside staff education and public communication. It is an institutional example, not a measured intervention-effect study.

**Activity:** Choose a bounded U.S. area in the profiler, then use [the capstone](../content/10_capstone.ipynb) to prepare a hypothetical interagency meeting. Assign responsibility for EO quality, population interpretation, water sampling, animal observations, health evidence and community engagement. Keep the proposed arrangement separate from the historical account.

**Deliverable:** A meeting agenda, evidence-sharing plan and decision question. Describe validation and opportunities to revise the question. Do not treat a descriptive map as evidence of health improvement or assume facilities described as proposed in the source have been built.

## EPA water research: choosing the right boundary

**Read:** The HAB and watershed sections of [EPA's research-example collection](https://www.epa.gov/one-health/one-health-case-studies-and-examples).

EPA describes environmental monitoring and collaboration with CDC on HAB reporting. The collection also points to environmental AMR monitoring, EnviroAtlas, wastewater surveillance and wildfire-smoke resources.

**Activity:** Compare [population boundaries](../content/02_population.ipynb), [spectral proxies](../content/04_vegetation_water.ipynb) and [spatial joins](../content/07_eo_population_joins.ipynb). Contrast a rectangle, watershed, monitoring site, Census tract and service area. Any future dataset needs independently verified public-domain status, dates, metadata and credits.

**Deliverable:** A boundary-and-denominator comparison and validation plan. Distinguish environmental measurements, animal observations and reported human illness. A water index is not a toxin result, and unreported illness is not zero illness. EPA's linked products and partner datasets remain external resources.
